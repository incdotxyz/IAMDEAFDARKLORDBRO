Defense Statement · DarkLordCG

In Defense of DarkLordCG

Ladies and gentlemen, let me start with the thing this whole document is built to make you forget.

DarkLordCG has not been accused of a single thing that has been proven.

Not one. Read the report again. Every claim that would actually matter — the copied source code, the reused scripts, the tracking, the Jenny Mod recreation — lives in a section titled "Reported but not fully verified." The author wrote the accusation in the headline and the admission in the footnotes, and he's hoping you only read the headline.

So let me do what this report refuses to do. Let me sort the facts from the insinuations.

One — using AI is not a crime, and this report knows it.

DarkLord said openly he uses AI to help develop. He said it publicly. He didn't hide it. And the report's own Section 2 says there's "an important difference between independently developing similar functionality and reproducing another developer's actual protected work." Correct. So where's the evidence he crossed that line? There isn't any. The report says the difference exists, then spends the next six sections acting like he crossed it. You can't have it both ways.

Two — the room database is public information.

Room names. Room IDs. Player counts. Capacity. Ping. Availability. Every one of those things is on the lobby screen of every player who opens Chicken Gun. The report calls this a "privacy concern." It's not. It's the TV guide. You can't accuse a man of surveillance for reading what the game hands him on a plate.

Three — the nickname file. The report admits it couldn't even read it.

Section 3, in writing: "Its full contents could not be retrieved during this review, so its exact structure and total number of records remain unconfirmed."

So the author doesn't know what's in the file. Doesn't know its structure. Doesn't know how many records. And then builds two sections of privacy alarm around it anyway. That's not a finding. That's a guess wearing a suit.

Four — the Jenny Mod section. And I want you to look closely here, because this is where it stops being a report and starts being a hit piece.

A Telegram screenshot. Removed "for security reasons." Which means nobody can check it. Framed with "alleged," "reported," "described as." One unverifiable screenshot, dropped without context, sitting right next to a game with a young player base.

That's not concern for players. That's a weapon dressed as a safety pamphlet. And the author knows it — that's why he buried it under "should be treated seriously" while admitting in the same breath that it's unverified. If there's real evidence, publish it and let it be examined. If there isn't, take it out. You don't accuse a man in paragraph six and call it "alleged" in paragraph seven and pretend those cancel out.

Five — decompilation. Even the report admits it's not automatically illegal.

And then treats it like a confession for the rest of the document. If the concern is copyright, the standard is what was copied and how it was used — and the report gives you zero concrete copied artifacts. Zero. Not one file, not one line, not one comparison. "Potential." "Possible." "May." Nine times. That's not evidence. That's a weather forecast.

Six — and this is the part that should bother you most.

The report's own "publicly documented" list — the things it actually stands behind — contains no wrongdoing at all. A person uses AI. A public repo exists. A public room list contains public data. That's it. That's the entire documented case.

Everything else — the tracking, the Jenny Mod claim, the source-code copying — is unverified, unreadable, or a screenshot that was conveniently deleted before anyone could check it. The report builds its evidence on one list and gets its outrage from another. Those are two different lists. Don't let him mix them.

So here's where we land.

DarkLordCG used AI. That's allowed. He published public game data. That's not a crime. He's been accused of copying protected material, and the report that accuses him admits it doesn't have the evidence. What he actually did — openly, publicly, without hiding — is build something for a community he's part of, using tools anyone can use, from data anyone can see.

If ChaloApps has a real concern, they can reach out directly, like adults, with actual evidence. That's the honest path. But what's happening here isn't that. This is a man's name dragged through a document full of "alleged" and "reported" and "unconfirmed," with the conclusion doing all the accusing while the evidence section quietly shrugs.

DarkLord doesn't need to prove his innocence. The report needed to prove his guilt, and it didn't. Not once.

Publish the evidence, or drop the accusations. Those are the only two honest options.

And if the author wants to argue the point — we'll be right here.
